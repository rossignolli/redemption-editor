Record, edit, export

Everything that happened, played back on cue

The mod records every person, horse, wagon and prop near you as timestamped data, then rebuilds the scene on stand-ins you can pause, rewind and film from any angle.

01

Record

Nothing to remember. The buffer keeps the last minute, and a manual recording runs as long as you need.

  • Always-on buffer. The last 60 seconds stay in memory (set the length and memory in Replay Settings). Shift+F4 saves them as a clip.
  • Manual recording. F4 marks a start, F4 again saves everything since. No time limit: long takes are saved in parts, with a notice each time a part is saved.
  • Every body in the scene. People, horses, animals, wagons, trains, weapons, props and reins, with the full skeleton pose of each one, and the muscles and sweat of every horse.
  • How they looked. Outfits, weapon components, hats knocked off mid-fight, wagon paint and livery.
  • Sound and effects. Gunshots with muzzle flash and the weapon's own sound, speech in the original voice, your character's grunts and pain sounds, footsteps, punches, impacts, blood, arrows.
  • The world around it. Doors, crates and barrels that move or break, grass that bends as horses pass, plus the time of day and weather of the clip.
  • Named for the place. Clips are saved as 042-Valentine, with a thumbnail, region, weather and character in the gallery.
02

Edit

An editor overlay (F2) with a painted menu in the style of the game's own, a clip gallery, transport controls and a camera timeline.

  • Transport. Pause, scrub back and forward, step frame by frame, playback speed from 5% to 200%.
  • Free camera. Fly anywhere in the replay with WASD, QE and the mouse.
  • Camera markers. Keyframes on the timeline, each one Free, Game (the camera you actually played with), Target (aim at a rider) or Follow, blended or cut, with fades.
  • Start and end markers bound every clip; no marker can cross them.
  • Time and weather. Change the time of day and the weather of any clip, buffer clips included.
  • Clean frame. Game HUD hidden during playback, your real character moved out of the shot and restored afterwards.
  • Projects. Compose several clips in order: trim, split at the playhead, move, duplicate, delete, and play or scrub the whole project. Up to 512 clips and 30 minutes.
  • Keys on screen. A key bar always shows what works in the current screen, and every key can be changed.
  • Your size. Replay Settings are grouped (Recording, Playback, Storage, Interface), and the interface scales from 80 to 125%.
03

Export

Render the edit to a video file inside the game, frame by frame, with no screen recorder.

  • Locked clock. The game advances a fixed step per frame, so every frame is rendered and none is dropped, even at 4K.
  • GPU pipeline. Captured and color-converted on the GPU, encoded on the GPU (NVIDIA NVENC, AMD AMF or Intel Quick Sync) to H.264, H.265 10-bit or AV1 10-bit.
  • Masters. ProRes 422 HQ, DNxHR HQX 10-bit, FFV1, lossless H.265 or a PNG sequence.
  • Real motion blur. 8, 16 or 32 sub-frames per frame, with a shutter angle from 90° to 360°.
  • Look. Letterbox (2.39, 2.00, 1.85), film grain and your own .cube LUT.
  • Finishing. Camera markers become chapters, a thumbnail is saved next to the file, and an audio pass adds the game sound.

Under the hood

Not a video. The scene itself.

A replay here is data, not pixels. Each entity is stored as packets on one timeline and played back on inert stand-ins, so you can move the camera after the fact.

Scrubbing backwards re-creates and re-places whatever existed at that moment, and blood and debris come and go with the playhead. Clips are versioned files that refuse to load on a different game build instead of guessing.

Export

Motion blur the way a film camera sees it

The exporter renders several sub-frames for each video frame and blends them in linear light on the GPU, so wheels and hooves smear the way a real shutter would.

Pick a codec, keep the frame

Time to render one 4K video frame, measured in game. Without blur, export runs close to real time. With blur, each sample is a full game frame, so 8 seconds with 16 samples takes about 2 minutes.

Pipeline

Locked clock
The game's timer is stepped exactly once per sample, so the world moves at the same speed with or without blur. In testing, every 8-second export came out with exactly 480 frames.
GPU capture
The back buffer is copied and converted to NV12, P010 or 10-bit RGB on the GPU, then handed straight to the encoder. No CPU color conversion.
Encoders
H.264, H.265 10-bit and AV1 10-bit on the GPU (NVENC, AMF or Quick Sync), a half-size preview, ProRes 422 HQ, DNxHR HQX, FFV1, lossless H.265 4:4:4 and PNG.
Frame rates
24, 25, 30, 50, 60, 120, 29.97 and 59.94 fps. Export the whole clip or only the stretch between the start and end markers.
Sound
A second pass plays the clip in real time and records only the game's audio, aligned to the picture within one frame at 60 fps in testing.
Clean picture
No HUD, prompts, notices or editor UI end up in the file. A queue, chapters from your markers and a JPEG thumbnail come with every export.

Good to know: the export needs the game on the Vulkan renderer. FFmpeg comes in the download, nothing else to install.

How it works

From the saddle to the cutting room

  1. Play

    Ride, hunt, get into trouble. The buffer quietly keeps the last minute of everything around you.

    Always on, or F4 to mark a start

  2. Save

    Keep the moment as a clip in ReplayClips, named after the place where it started.

    Shift+F4 saves the last 60 s

  3. Direct

    Open the editor, play the clip, scrub to the moment and lay down camera markers with the free camera.

    F2 editor, F11 free camera

  4. Export

    Choose a format, blur and look, and let the game render the film frame by frame.

    Ctrl+E export dialog

The design: state is recorded per entity and replayed on stand-ins. Each engine hook checks that the game code matches what it expects and switches itself off, with a line in the log, when it does not.

Requirements and install

One ZIP into the game folder

Requirements

Game
Red Dead Redemption 2 for PC, Story Mode
Platform
PC, game build 1.0.1491.50 (the current update). On another build the mod tells you on screen, and features that don't match switch themselves off.
Prerequisites

ScriptHookRDR2. By Alexander Blade: ScriptHookRDR2.dll and the ASI loader dinput8.dll in the game folder, for the current game update.

ReShade 6.6.2 to 6.8.x, "with full add-on support". Installed for RDR2.exe with the API the game uses (Vulkan or DirectX 12). Without ReShade the recording still works, but the editor does not open.

Disk space for clips
Clips are saved in the game folder. A minute in a busy town can take a few hundred MB; clips you don't open for a week are compressed in the background.
Renderer for export
The game on Vulkan. GPU presets need NVIDIA NVENC, AMD AMF or Intel Quick Sync; the CPU formats work on any PC.
Version
1.0.0, free and open source (GPL-3.0).

Install

  1. Close the game. If you had a test build, delete its files first.
  2. Copy everything in the ZIP into the folder with RDR2.exe: RedemptionEditor.asiRedemptionEditor.addon64RedemptionEditorCore.dllRedemptionEditor.iniRedemptionEditor\
  3. Start the game and load Story Mode. ScriptHookRDR2.log should list RedemptionEditor.asi, and RedemptionEditor.log appears next to it.
  4. Uninstall by deleting those files and the RedemptionEditor folder (and ReplayClips and ReplayProjects if you don't want your clips).

FAQ

Questions at the campfire

Does it work in Red Dead Online?

No. Redemption Editor is for Story Mode only and never touches online mode. Do not use it in Red Dead Online.

How much performance does recording cost?

Measured in game in a scene with dozens of ranchers and horses: about 0.32 ms per frame of mod time while recording in the normal configuration, and about 0.72 ms with the developer overlay switched on. Frame time at the 95th percentile rose by roughly 0.09 ms and the frame rate stayed at 60 fps (VSync on).

In an 18-minute session parked in Valentine with the town's population around, the 60-second buffer stayed between 65 and 110 MB of memory, under its 128 MB budget.

Do I have to remember to press record?

No. With the buffer on, the mod always keeps the last 60 seconds. Press Shift+F4 after something happens and it becomes a clip. For longer takes, F4 starts a manual recording with no time limit.

Where are my clips?

In <game folder>\ReplayClips, named with a counter and the place where the recording started, for example 042-Valentine.rpclip (shown as "042 Valentine" in the editor). Exported videos go to Documents\Rockstar Games\Red Dead Redemption 2\Videos\Redemption Editor\ by default.

Will it break after a game update?

Every engine hook checks that the game code matches what it was built for. On a different build, the affected feature switches itself off and the log says which one, instead of guessing. Clips recorded on another game build are refused rather than loaded wrong.

Is anything not recorded yet?

A few things: water is not recorded (the game's own water runs in the replay), and during a mission the world is only hidden, not cleared, so mission characters can show up. Props another mod placed before a replay are cleared when it starts (ones placed during the replay stay). Things added in a later version, like horse muscles, only show on clips recorded with that version.

Does export need a specific GPU?

The GPU presets (H.264, H.265, AV1) use NVIDIA NVENC, AMD AMF or Intel Quick Sync; AV1 needs a recent card (RTX 40 or 50, Radeon RX 7000, Arc). ProRes, DNxHR, FFV1 and PNG are encoded on the CPU. Export needs the game on the Vulkan renderer; on DirectX 12 it does not run.

Does it send anything anywhere?

No. The mod has no telemetry and never connects to the internet. A report ZIP (Shift+F1) stays on your disk until you send it yourself.

Which version of the game?

Story Mode on PC, game build 1.0.1491.50. Tested on Steam. The Epic Games Store and Rockstar Games Launcher versions should work too: if you play on one of them, tell us how it went in a GitHub issue.

Found a bug?

Press Shift+F1 in game to save a report ZIP and attach it to an issue on GitHub. Support happens there only, so nothing gets lost. Mark the moment with Shift+F2 first when a replay looks or sounds wrong.

Where do I get it?

On Nexus Mods and on the GitHub releases page, with the full source code.
